# Week 4 Subject: Church is my new family

## Lesson 1 Why go to church?

> Read Hebrews 10:25

Today, we have a new subject: church. What do you know about church? Many people think it is a beautiful building or a collection of various rituals and traditions. But we need to know what God thinks about it (see Week 1, Lesson 2) and what the Bible tells us about church.

We just read Hebrews 10:25, which says that a church is an assembly of people. “What do we call the building we see?” you may ask. It is a temple.

center: <b>Let us summarize:</b>
1) <u>The building</u> where people gather to pray and worship God, with all its decorations and rituals, is a <b>temple</b>.
2) <u>The people</u> who gather to pray and worship God are a <b>church</b>.

Acts 2:46 says that the disciples of Jesus gathered:
1) In a temple;
2) In their homes.

We also need to join the church in a temple (Sunday worship) and our homes (during the week). Both places are very important, but we will talk about Sunday service in this lesson. Every Sunday morning, a Sunday service takes place. All people who belong to the local church gather together. The Bible says that we should not abandon (skip) services.
Is it possible to receive salvation without a church? What if you believe in your heart, pray quietly, do good things, read the Bible, and not sin? Practically, it is not possible. <i>(We are not considering exceptions: uninhabited islands, spaceships, etc.)</i>. The Bible says we are the members of the Body of Christ, that is, the Church (1 Corinthians 12).
Imagine your finger decided to separate itself from the body and live on its own. How long will it last? Several minutes, perhaps. What if the head separates? Nothing good will come of it. The body can only live if its members stay together! You are not a church on your own but only together with other people!
Therefore, there is no reason not to go to church. The only exceptions are conditions that do not depend on us (serious illness, work shift). Church is a ship in the middle of the sea. You are either on board or overboard!

## Lesson 2 Home church is a spiritual family

> Read Acts 2:46

Now we will talk about church gatherings in homes or <b>home church</b>. Sometimes, it is also called a home group or cell.

What is the purpose of a home church? Its primary purpose is to have fellowship and serve one another and the world.

The main church has teaching (sermons) of Apostles (pastors, preachers), worship, and strong prayer. This is also where many people receive Jesus (repent), and many are healed.

center: <b>What takes place in a home church, then?</b>

1. In a home church, people have fellowship with one another (there are 5-10 people in a home church);
2. In a home church, people drink tea together to form a natural, family atmosphere;
3. In a home church, people serve one another with counsel, prayer, gifts of the Holy Spirit, and support;
4. In a home church, people grow by sharing insights from Sunday service sermons;
5. In a home church, people worship God in songs, prayers, and gifts of the Holy Spirit;
6. In a home church, people serve non-believers by helping them come to Christ.

A home church is a spiritual family. It has spiritual parents and children. It is very important for you to become a part of a home church and love it.

image: img_cover_01.png

A home church usually lasts for 1,5-2 hours. Everyone brings some food (if possible). You can bring children, relatives, and friends to a home church.

Home Church

Even if you come for the first time, you can participate in the life of a home church. Home church members can take turns hosting home church meetings, if possible. It does not matter what apartment, furniture, or plates and dishes you have. A home church is a gathering not to observe but to serve. Remember that. There is one more important thing I want to say. Nobody needs anyone in the world today. IN A HOME CHURCH, YOU NEED PEOPLE AND PEOPLE NEED YOU. You will become an <u>individual</u>, one that is needed, loved, and appreciated. Value your home church!

## Lesson 3 What happens in a church and why?

> Read 1 Corinthians 14:26

What was your impression about your first visit to the church? “It was a little shocking” would be a likely response.
Let us look at what occurs during a church meeting (it is similar to what occurs in a home church).

center: <b>Praise and worship</b>

People rejoice and sing a lot. Singing can be provisionally divided into two parts: <b><u>praise</u></b> and <b><u>worship</u></b>. Praise is exalting God as the King and Lord. This is God’s etiquette: God receives our prayers if we praise and thank him. Praise is joyful, and worship is quiet and more intimate. Do not feel bad if you are confused. The ability to distinguish between praise and worship and enter into God’s presence comes with time. It is a process. If you are sincere and steady, you will gradually learn all this.

center: <b>Sermon</b>

<b><u>A sermon</u></b> by the church pastor or other ministers takes much of the meeting’s time. A sermon is ministering with the Word. From it, we get knowledge, revelations that help us solve our problems, and a deeper understanding of God. Having a notepad to write down passages from the Scripture and insights you liked is helpful. Then, you will be able to share the sermon with others more accurately.

center: <b>Prayer</b>

At the church, people pray a lot. <b><u>Prayer</u></b> is a conversation with God. There are different kinds of prayer. Some prayers glorify God. In others, we present our needs to God or pray for the needs of other people.

center: <b>Special ministries</b>

There are also <b><u>special ministries</u></b> at the church, such as various announcements, testimonies, offerings (we will talk about them during the next lesson), etc.
May all of this serve our growth.
One newly converted Christian told me, “I agree to serve God, but I do not agree with how church meetings are conducted. Some people pray too loud, and some sing too joyfully. It should be quiet and calm”. It reminded me of one brother, a preacher, who is with the Lord now. He loved to sing very much. He sang with the church, with the choir, and with the soloists. It would have been fine, but he had a loud, raspy voice and was completely tone-deaf. If someone told him, “Why do you sing? You impede everyone”, he would answer, “God has many birds, there are nightingales and crows, and God allows each one of them to sing according to its abilities.” He did not stop to praise God with his raspy voice until he died! Every person has the right to praise God and pray the way God reveals to them. If you are a quiet person, pray quietly. If you like loudness, pray loudly.
Every person has the right to serve God in accordance with their personality. The church is for everyone, young and old. Respect and love one another because we all are God’s family.

## Lesson 4 Offerings and tithe

> Read 1 Corinthians 16:1-2

For many, this is the most challenging issue in serving God. Many people are ready to give their lives for God but do not want to give a single coin.

Once, a preaching teacher from the US visited my home. He was of Italian descent. He told me a story. In some old Italian churches, people do not like to listen to sermons about tithe, while the same cannot be said about American churches (it is no surprise that the Italian lira was devalued so many times). One pastor from Italy invited him to speak at his church and warned him that the people would not accept a sermon about tithe. But God inspired him to speak specifically about tithe! He resisted, but God’s Spirit insisted, and he gave up. There was an ominous silence during the sermon. Many people demonstratively left the meeting at the end. No one shook this brother’s hand. He was shocked. What a shame!

A few years later, he visited that church again. A young man came to him and invited him to his home for lunch. When they arrived, he was surprised to see a beautiful house. He was shown into the garage and saw two beautiful cars there. The young man asked him, “Do you remember your sermon about tithe?” “Oh, yes!” he answered, “it was the most terrible sermon in my life”. “No,” the young man said, “it was the best sermon. I had about $20 with me on that day. God told me to tithe and continue to do so in the future. I resisted, but I did it. One week later, God helped me open my own business, and I made a lot of money in two years. What you see now is only a small part of what I own. I invited you to my home to thank you for your sermon”.
I do not want you to take this story as agitation.

center: <b>Why do we tithe (give ten percent of our income)?</b>

1) Because God said so in the Bible.
2) To protect ourselves from ruin and to receive financial blessings.
3) To prove our faithfulness to God with actions;
4) To support the needs of the church in preaching the Gospel.
5) It is God’s part in the financial sphere.

center: <b>Where does tithe go?</b>

1) To cover rent and all administrative expenses, including construction and repair of the church building.
2) To support the ministers and administrative staff working in the church full-time.
3) To reach the world with the Gospel (people needing help, missionaries, and evangelization).
4) To help members of the church who are in need.

As a church pastor, I want to tell you that I have nothing to do with money. The accounting office takes care of money in the church, and their accounting is strict. The office answers to the bank, tax inspection, and the church Counting Board.
God does not need tithe, and the pastor does not need tithe. We need it for God to bless us!
Also, do not forget that this is all done voluntarily, and you decide whether to give or not.

May the Lord bless you!

## Lesson 5 Mentorship

> Read Hebrews 13:17

Now, we will talk about something you already know a little about. The topic of today’s lesson is Mentorship.
First, let us define the terms: you are the one being mentored, and the one mentoring you is the mentor.
Who is a mentor?
First and foremost, they are your friend who came to the Lord earlier than you. A mentor is not a teacher or a pastor but your friend. A mentor listens more than they teach; they simply help you know Jesus better.

Once, I was watching alpinists on TV. They were linked to each other with ropes. After one of them climbed a little, he helped the next climber and then the next one. If someone stumbled, everybody held him. They climbed to the top together and celebrated their victory together. If an accident happens, they will deal with it together.
The best definition of mentorship is “friendship”, as it is, indeed, pure Christian friendship.

I want to note that a mentor has no right to decide for you. A mentor tells you about different solutions to difficult situations, but you have to make your own decisions. You are an individual and the right to make decisions belongs to you.
I want to ask you to respect your mentor and pray for them. Your mentor is an ordinary human who willingly sacrifices time and effort for your salvation.
There is the “Law of Generations”. We cannot return all the love, patience, money, sleepless nights, premature wrinkles, and sometimes even their lives to our parents, who devoted themselves to us. We must give it to our children! They give it to their children, and life on Earth continues.

You can not fully return your mentor’s help. There is only one way: to complete the Mentorship and become a mentor yourself! “But I know so little!” you may say. Remember, a mentor is not a teacher, but a friend who helps one walk along the way (using the book you are reading). And I hope you know what it means to be a friend.
Mentorship can also take place in groups of 5 to 25 people. In such a case, the mentor is an experienced minister or a pastor. It resembles a family with many children who all learn from their parents.
In such a case, you can bring your friends to these classes, complete lessons with them, and help them grow.
I would like to ask you something: prepare a surprise for your mentor. Pray about it and ask God for wisdom.  Show your mentor that you love and value them and their help.
