# Week 1 Subject: Difficult questions

## Lesson 1 Have I switched my faith?

> Let us read together a passage from the Bible, written in Ephesians 4:5  
> “One Lord, one faith, one baptism”.

When we receive Jesus Christ as our Lord, other people might accuse us of switching faith. We can experience feelings of guilt and feel like we betrayed someone. Let us explore this issue further.

Ephesians 4:5 says that there is one Lord, one faith, and one baptism. Then why are there so many churches, you may ask?

My parents have fourteen children. Almost all my brothers and sisters have their own families, and they are all different. They have different habits, education, and lifestyles. Some live in cities, and some live in villages. They have different jobs and characters. They dress differently, cook differently, and raise their children differently. But we all have something in common: our father and our mother. We all love our parents very much, and our parents love us.

Do you see my point here? Just like families, churches are, were, and will be different. But we all have something in common: our Lord Jesus Christ. He is our Spiritual Father. My faith is in God, and I love Him. It is not possible to switch faith because there is only one God. All Christians believe in the same God.

Once, I heard my four children arguing, and my youngest son cried, “Mother is mine!” The other two children shouted, “No, she is mine!” They were ready to fight when suddenly the oldest son said, “Wait, she is ours.”

We, the children of the Heavenly Father, must understand that we, as churches (families), are different. But our Father, our faith, and our love are one.

You cannot switch faith. You can either believe in God or not.

When I got married, I did not betray my mother. She always remained my mother. My mother is one person, and my wife is another. Church is one thing, and God is another. Never confuse God with the church. Thus, you did not switch faith; you have simply begun to believe.

## Lesson 2 What would people say?

> Let us read together a passage from the Bible, written in 1 Corinthians 4:3-4  
> “I care very little if I am judged by you or by any human court; indeed, I do not even judge myself. My conscience is clear, but that does not make me innocent. It is the Lord who judges me”.

Our entire life, we are trying to please other people. We often prepare dinner for our guests just to look good in front of our neighbors. We try to accommodate and please our co-workers, neighbors, friends, and relatives.

Apostle Paul writes in 1 Corinthians 4:3-4 that God’s opinion should be the most important for us, not people’s opinion. After they come to Jesus, people often worry: “What will my wife or husband say?”, “What about my girlfriend or boyfriend, my co-workers, or that caustic neighbor?” We do not know what to do, what to say to those people, and whether or not we should let them know about our decision to believe in Jesus.

Other people often say offensive things that hurt us, and we become slaves to their opinions.

Once, I traveled to Norway through Finland with my team on an evangelism trip. In Finland, we boarded a big Swedish passenger ship. Our Norwegian friends told us that our lower-class tickets did not include private rooms and food. We asked several workers on the ship to make sure, and the answer was always the same. So we just took places where we could, unpacked our food, and got ready to spend the night where we were. I dozed off in a chair on the upper deck. Suddenly, one of our team members woke me up and said that cabins and meals were available for all of us. I moved into my cabin. It was a good room, and the restaurant had plenty of food. It was all included in our tickets. I asked him how he learned about these wonderful accommodations. It turned out that he went to a senior officer on the ship, and that man permitted us to have food and accommodation with our tickets. Many people advised us on how to better accommodate ourselves on the deck, but it turned out that only the opinion of the senior officer was important to us.

The opinion of the person with wisdom and authority is most important.

For example, it is essential to know the doctor’s opinion about your health, a farmer’s opinion about seeds, and an architect’s opinion about construction, is it not? Can you imagine a musician assisting a surgeon during an operation or a farmer helping a computer programmer?

Life, death, eternity, and salvation belong to God’s wisdom and authority. It is only His opinion and His Word that matter.

## Lesson 3 Believing is not boring!

> Let us read together a passage from the Bible, written in Acts 5:12-16

“The apostles performed many signs and wonders among the people. And all the believers used to meet together in Solomon’s Colonnade. No one else dared join them, even though they were highly regarded by the people. Nevertheless, more and more men and women believed in the Lord and were added to their number. As a result, people brought the sick into the streets and laid them on beds and mats so that at least Peter’s shadow might fall on some of them as he passed by. Crowds gathered also from the towns around Jerusalem, bringing their sick and those tormented by impure spirits, and all of them were healed.”

Many people, especially young adults, are afraid to receive Jesus Christ, thinking that their lives will become boring. Many young people think that the Bible is a code of prohibitions.

In the early church, we see that Christian life was vibrant, joyful, and exciting without sin.

I want to assure you that there are no prohibitions in the Bible, only warnings and advice. The Bible is an instruction book for life and death. A sign on a bottle that says, “Caution: poison” is not a prohibition but a warning. Sin looks good only on the outside. Inside, it is poison. It leads to very difficult consequences for our lives, health, and finances and often even affects our children and grandchildren. The Bible does not limit us. It warns us.

Once, I was asked to check the quality of a concrete mixer’s electrical connection. Having an electrician diploma, I am well aware of the operating rules for electrical installations. I found many mistakes during the examination: bare wires, no ground connection, etc. Then, I told the operators that they should not use the concrete mixer. They mocked me, “Look at this newbie know-it-all!” and let me go. An hour later, I learned that one of the workers was killed. They did not want to follow the rules. Rules are not made for the young or the old, for the wise or the unwise. They are made for everybody. It is a warning. What would happen if no one followed the rules on the road? It would be total chaos.

Jesus Christ gives us a new, wonderful life full of His blessings. You may ask, “But what will I have instead of my loud friends and vibrant parties?” You will have a hundred times more. The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and you will have a very loud, joyful, but pure friendship of God.

Jesus Christ gives us new joy, friends, feelings, opportunities, a new meaning in life, forgiveness of sins, a cleansed conscience, renewed family, beautiful relationships with God and people, blessings in health and finances, and eternal life. You will not become bored with Him. It is a wonderful life.

## Lesson 4 Will I be able to do it?

> Find Ephesians 2:1-9 in the Bible and read it.

The passage says that all people follow someone’s will. It is either God’s will or the Devil’s. You will say: “No, I lived my way.”

Once, I talked to a chronic smoker. He said, “I can smoke, and I can stop smoking. I am free.” Then I told him, “You can only smoke. You are not able to quit. If you could, you would have done it a long time ago.” He was, in fact, a slave to cigarettes. The Bible says that a person who sins is a slave to sin. The Devil is the father of sin. A person who sins follows the Devil’s will. How many sins have enslaved us: anger, irritation, drunkenness, sexual immorality, lies? It is difficult to name them all.

You may say, “Exactly, that’s what I was afraid of. I would not be able to leave those things behind. I’ll first become a good person, and then I will come to God.”

NO! You will never be able to become good by yourself. You need to give yourself to God, and then He will give you power to overcome sin.

Let us remember:

1. We are not able to become good on our own.  
2. God has the power to overcome sin.  
3. God wants to give us this power.  

If you have a problem with your health, you need to see a doctor. You go to a hospital and entrust the doctor with your health. Who needs a doctor: the healthy or the sick? Who needs Jesus: the righteous or the sinners? If you entrust your life to Jesus, He will give you the power to overcome sin and various problems in His Name.

You will not overcome all of your sins at once. You will not become a super saint in one day. It is a learning process.

Every day, you will learn more and more about the ways you can use the power Jesus gave you. Do not despair if you do not succeed on your first try. You will do it on your second or third attempt. You will definitely do it.

Jesus gave us power, but it is not our power but God’s power.

You can do anything with Jesus.

## Lesson 5 Not everyone is worth following!

> Find Hebrews 12:1-2 in the Bible and read it.

Many people look at Christians who sin and are tempted by them. Questions arise, like “Why is this person doing that while another person is doing this?” Let us find out.

We are not called “Peterians,” “Johnians,” or “Philippians.” We are called Christians, and Jesus Christ is our only ideal.

It is written in the Bible that there is no person who does not sin. It is the teacher that students learn from, not other students. All students make mistakes, but the teacher is the one who teaches. He sets an example, and our example is Jesus. He did not sin.

Read about Him in the four Gospels. What a life. Jesus faced so many trials and temptations, but there was not a single sin in His life.

Do not look at people. Look at Jesus.

Once, I was giving a lift to a man who was a non-believer. We started talking about God. He told me, “I would have come to God, but I knew a Christian who stole from our local government. That’s why I will not do it. And you are all like him.” My response was, “Maybe that person stole, maybe some other Christians have committed even bigger sins, but I don’t believe in people. I believe in Jesus. Jesus never stole or lied.” When we started talking about Jesus, that man began to cry.

Never make excuses for other people, never judge them, and never focus on people. Look only at Jesus.

Once, my daughter asked me to teach her how to ride a bicycle. She would ride it as long as I ran alongside her and held the bicycle. But as soon as I let go, she would fall. I got tired and sat down to rest. Then, suddenly, I realized how I could teach her. I said, “Look forward, and do not look down at the wheel. Hold the handlebar and steer, aiming forward.” And she did it.

Do not look down at your feet – there is a lot of dirt there. Look onward at Jesus. Then you will definitely get to your destination.

Always look to Jesus.  
Always learn from Jesus.  
Always believe Jesus.
